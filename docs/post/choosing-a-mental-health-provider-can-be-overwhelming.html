Choosing a Mental Health Provider Can Be Overwhelming

Choosing a therapist can feel overwhelming.

The mental health profession can be surprisingly difficult to navigate. How do you know the difference between a psychologist, psychiatrist, counselor, therapist, primary care provider (PCP) or nurse practitioner? Who does what? More importantly, how do you know who is the right fit for you?

Then there are all the terms you'll hear, like "therapy modality." Simply put, a therapy modality is the approach or method a therapist uses to help people heal and grow. Most people don't realize there are many different approaches to therapy, and why would they? Unless you work in the field, no one expects you to know this.

By the time many people reach out for help, they're already feeling overwhelmed, anxious, discouraged or emotionally exhausted. The last thing they want is to feel like they need a degree in mental health just to choose a therapist. Sometimes clients leave an initial consultation thinking, "I have no idea what half of those words meant."

If that sounds familiar, you're not alone.

While I can't eliminate all of the confusion, I can offer three practical tips to help you feel more confident as you begin your search.

3 Helpful Tips

  1. Don't focus on the letters after the name first. Focus on whether the provider treats what you're struggling with. Heck, you can google those.
  2. Ask how the therapist actually conducts therapy, rather than just asking what modalities they use.
  3. Pay attention to how you feel during the consultation. Research consistently shows that the quality of the therapeutic relationship is one of the strongest predictors of positive outcomes, regardless of the specific therapy model used.
